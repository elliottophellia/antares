# Writing clearly

## Lead with the answer

The first sentence says what happened or what to do. Background comes after,
and only as much as the reader needs to act. Most people stop after the first
paragraph — make sure that is enough.

## Cut what carries no weight

Delete: "it's important to note", "in order to", "basically", "simply",
"leverage", "utilize", "robust", "seamless", "in today's world".

Prefer the shorter, plainer word every time: use not utilize, help not
facilitate, about not approximately, so not therefore.

## Be specific

"Improved performance" tells nobody anything. "Cut import time from 40s to 3s
by batching the writes" tells them what changed and why to believe it.

Numbers, names, and file paths beat adjectives.

## Structure

- Short paragraphs, one idea each.
- A list only when the items are genuinely parallel. Prose is better for
  reasoning that flows.
- Headings that say something, not "Overview" and "Details".

## Tone

Write as one competent person to another. No hype, no apology, no exclamation
marks. Say what you did and what you are unsure about — hedging everything
reads the same as hedging nothing.

## Before sending

Read it once as the recipient. Does the first line answer the question they
have? Is there anything they must do, and is it obvious? Cut a further 10%.
